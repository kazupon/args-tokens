| `passthrough` _(optional)_    | `boolean` | Whether to give the arguments that the schema does not take, such as for a CLI that passes them on to another program. When `true`, the result has `passthroughIndices`: the indexes, in the arguments given to `parseArgs()`, of the arguments that the schema does not take, in ascending order. They are an option that is not in the schema, with the argument that it takes as its value: as without `passthrough`, it takes the next argument when it has no value of its own, such as `--bail=3`, and that argument is not an option or `--`. A short option group with an option that is not in the schema is given whole, as it was given, such as `-qk` with `q` in the schema, whose options in the schema are still resolved. Without `shortGrouping`, a group is its first option, with the other letters as its value, so it is given only when that option is not in the schema. A positional argument that no positional argument of the schema takes, after the ones up to `skipPositional`, and the arguments after `--`, but not `--` itself, are given too. An option of the schema, the argument that it takes as its value, and a positional argument that the schema takes are not given. The indexes are given even when there are validation errors, as `values` are. `parse()` also gives the arguments at the indexes, as `passthrough`. The indexes are the `index` of the tokens. With `allowCompatible`, `parseArgs()` splits a short option group with `-` in it, such as `-p-5`, at an option terminator, as `node:util` does, and the tokens after it have indexes past the arguments that they come from: the indexes after the group can reach past the last argument. `parse()` leaves those out, which leaves the indexes of the arguments after the group; the pieces of the group after its `-` are not given. Without it, the indexes are not collected, and the result has no `passthroughIndices`, as the result of `parseArgs()` of `node:util` has `tokens` only with its `tokens` option. **Default:** `false` |
| `shortGrouping` _(optional)_  | `boolean` | Whether to group short arguments. When `true`, each letter of a short option group is an option, and a value goes to the last one: `-vp 5` is `-v` and `-p` with `5`. When `false`, the other letters of a group are the value of its first option, which then does not take the next argument: `-p5 file` is `-p` with `5` and the positional `file`, and `-nfoo=bar` is `-n` with `foo=bar`. A boolean first option ignores the other letters, so `-vs` is only `-v`. **Default:** `false`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
